What Fed days do to SOXL and MU

The Fed almost never surprises on the decision itself, so markets trade the guidance. Fed days make SOXL jumpier, and most of the extra movement lands after the 2pm statement. But the direction has been a coin flip, and over the past three years the day's move usually reversed within a week. The pattern that matters most for tomorrow: after the 19 hikes since 2016, SOXL's median next month was −8.5%, compared with +5.1% after all meetings. MU barely noticed.

“Beats random” is how often the Fed-meeting average beat the averages from 10,000 random draws of the same number of ordinary days. Near 50% is noise; above 95% or below 5% would be notable. 4 · Rate hikes

All 19 hikes since 2016

Every hike was priced in beforehand. What differed was the backdrop. In 2016–17, hikes came during a semis boom and were mostly shrugged off (3 of 4 were higher a month later). In 2018 and 2022, hikes landed in falling markets, and the month after was usually worse. The first hike of a cycle (March 2022) rallied on the day and was down 29% a month later.

Four ways tomorrow can go

The hike-versus-hold split follows futures pricing. How the hike gets read is my judgement, based on the dot-plot debate and the reported vote count. The history under each scenario is the closest set of past meetings, using the 2-year yield move to classify tone. Samples are small, so read them as base rates, not forecasts.

True in every scenario

  • Expect a big intraday swing. SOXL's 1:30pm-to-close move on Fed days has been about 2.3× a normal afternoon, and its 20-day volatility is already at the 80th percentile of past meetings. A 5%+ swing after 2pm would be ordinary.
  • Don't trust the first hour. Over the past three years, SOXL's Fed-day move tended to reverse over the following week (rank correlation −0.40).
  • SOXL is entering washed out. After SOXL fell 10% or more into a meeting (13 cases), the next five days were higher 69% of the time (median +7.1%). The following month was usually weaker (median −5.7%). Tomorrow's 10-day slide is −9.2%, right at that line.
  • MU is a different animal. It sits 48% above its 200-day average and only 24% below its high, and its moves after hikes have been far milder than SOXL's. Earnings on 30 Sep will probably matter more for MU's month than the Fed.
  • The semis story can drown out the Fed. In the past three years, the biggest Fed-week moves came with chip news: NVDA +13% on 31 Jul 2024, and the −16% chip rout on 29 Jul 2026. Yesterday's AI-pacing selloff is that kind of driver.
Method & caveats

How this was measured

  1. Meetings. Decision dates come from the Fed's FOMC calendar. Rate moves come from FRED's upper target (DFEDTARU), which changes the day after the decision. The two March 2020 emergency cuts are excluded from all statistics.
  2. Prices. Yahoo daily bars (split/dividend adjusted) from 2015 for SOXL, MU, SOXX, QQQ and TLT, plus rate and dollar proxies. For the announcement window, Yahoo 60-minute bars from October 2023: the 12:30 bar's close stands in for 1:30pm, and the 15:30 bar's close is the 4pm close.
  3. Surprise. The decision matched pre-meeting pricing at every scheduled meeting in the sample except September 2024 (50bp against split pricing). Tone is measured by the daily change in the 2-year Treasury yield (FRED DGS2): +4bp or more counts as hawkish, −4bp or less as dovish. That move also absorbs other same-day news (CPI on 12 Jun 2024, the NYCB plunge on 31 Jan 2024). The three-year table also shows a hand-labelled read of each meeting from news coverage.
  4. Factors on the eve. RSI(14), distance from the 50/200-day averages, 10-day return, 20-day volatility, 1-year drawdown and VIX. Macro data uses only what was published before the meeting (CPI lagged 45 days, core PCE 60 days, unemployment 35 days). The real rate is the upper target minus core CPI y/y. MU/NVDA earnings timing comes from Yahoo earnings dates.
  5. Limits. 84 meetings over ten years, 24 in the three-year focus. Returns over 21 days overlap between meetings eight weeks apart only slightly, but market regimes (2018, 2022, 2025–26) dominate the long-horizon numbers. No trading costs.
  6. Scenario probabilities are judgement, anchored on futures pricing for hike vs hold. This is a historical study, not investment advice.
